# GTWorld Bank Transfer Flow Redesign

A conceptual UX/UI redesign of GTWorld's bank transfer experience, focused on reducing transfer errors, improving recipient verification, strengthening transaction confirmation, and creating a clearer, more confidence-inspiring transfer journey for Nigerian mobile banking users.

> **Project Type:** Personal UX/UI Design Project  
> **Programme:** 3MTT NextGen Programme  
> **Designer:** John Oyewole  
> **Status:** Completed Conceptual Prototype

---

## Project Overview

Bank transfers are a frequent and high-stakes activity for mobile banking users. A simple mistake in selecting a beneficiary, entering an amount, or confirming transaction details can result in financial loss, uncertainty, and a difficult recovery process.

This project explores a conceptual redesign of GTWorld's bank transfer experience. Rather than redesigning the entire banking application, the project focuses specifically on the transfer journey and the critical moments where users need clarity and confidence before sending money.

---

## The Problem

The existing transfer experience can require users to rely heavily on their own attention when verifying important transaction details.

Based on a review of the existing transfer flow and publicly available user feedback, key opportunities were identified around:

- Recipient verification
- Information hierarchy
- Transaction confirmation
- Error communication
- Beneficiary selection
- Transaction feedback

The project therefore focuses on improving the experience before, during, and immediately after a transfer is authorised.

---

## Project Goal

The goal is to create a clearer and more confidence-inspiring transfer experience that helps users:

- Verify the correct recipient before sending money.
- Easily identify important transaction details.
- Review transaction information before authorisation.
- Understand the outcome of a transaction.
- Recover more easily when something goes wrong.

---

## Target Users

The primary target users are Nigerian mobile banking customers who regularly make bank transfers.

The redesign particularly considers users who:

- Frequently send money through mobile banking.
- May multitask while making transactions.
- Need to quickly verify recipient details.
- Want reassurance before authorising a payment.
- Cannot afford costly transfer mistakes.

---

## Research & UX Audit

The project began with an audit of the existing GTWorld transfer flow and an analysis of publicly available user reviews.

The research identified recurring themes around:

### Transfer Confidence
Users need greater reassurance that the correct recipient and transaction details have been selected.

### Beneficiary Management
Users may experience difficulty identifying, selecting, or managing saved beneficiaries.

### Error Communication
Generic error messages can leave users uncertain about what happened and what action to take next.

### Transaction Feedback
Clear transaction status and confirmation are important after a transfer has been initiated.

### Application Reliability
Some reported issues relate to technical reliability, such as crashes, login problems, delayed transactions, and connectivity. These were acknowledged during research but are outside the scope of this conceptual UX redesign.

Key Design Decisions
1. Stronger Recipient Verification

Recipient information is presented more clearly before the user proceeds with the transaction.

Important information includes:

Recipient name
Bank
Account number
New recipient status where applicable

This helps users confirm that they are sending money to the intended recipient.

2. Improved Information Hierarchy

Critical transaction information receives stronger visual emphasis, particularly:

Recipient
Amount
Bank
Account number

Secondary information is visually separated to make the screen easier to scan.

3. Improved Review & Confirmation

The review stage is treated as a critical decision point rather than simply another step in the flow.

Users are encouraged to verify the transaction details before authorising the transfer.

4. Clearer Transaction Feedback

Success and error states are designed to communicate what happened and provide users with clear next steps.

Design Principles

The redesign follows four core principles:

Design for Confidence
Help users feel certain before committing to a financial transaction.

Prevent Errors Before They Happen
Give users opportunities to identify mistakes before money is sent.

Reduce Cognitive Load
Make important information easy to find, understand, and verify.

Communicate Clearly
Use straightforward language to explain system status and next steps.
